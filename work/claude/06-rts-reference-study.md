## Frontline Command interpretation

Use a connected military command console, not a website header, navigation rail
and dashboard. A compact resource strip should join a right production panel;
the lower selection tray should appear purposeful and leave a large view of the
world. Prefer machined edges, recessed wells, occasional bolts/seams and worn
paint over uniform rounded boxes. Frame detail must remain quiet behind labels.

Charcoal and warm gunmetal are the main materials. Olive paint, aged brass and
restrained amber are functional accents. Emergency red belongs to actual danger.
Blue/cyan chrome, neon gradients, floating analytics cards, empty decorative
gauges, giant marketing headings and generic pill buttons are rejected.

Create a recognizable original Frontline insignia, faction seals, distinctive
unit/building portraits and mission-map art. Each faction should feel authored
through silhouettes and material details. Production tiles need actual art,
cost/queue/progress states and clear lock explanations. Fonts should feel like a
military command device while remaining readable at1280×720 and enlarged scale.

The battlefield itself must carry the identity: layered terrain, roads, supply
yards, small environmental details, contact shadows, distinctive construction
silhouettes and controlled effects. Zoom must preserve visual hierarchy. A brown
grid with primitive vehicles and a polished border is insufficient.

## Required review evidence

Produce revised concepts using real current asset samples, then actual gameplay
screenshots at1600×900 and1280×720. Inspect both full frame and native-size crops
of sidebar, minimap, selected units, build tiles and terrain. Compare at least:

- Battlefield area and unobstructed tactical view.
- Console silhouette, material treatment and faction identity.
- Illustration quality and useful density of production buttons.
- Readable hierarchy, contrast, focus, warning and queue states.
- Consistent elevated camera, shadows and hull/turret registration.
- A real build/train/select/move/attack interaction and its visible feedback.

Record concrete differences and fixes in a Markdown mismatch ledger. A palette
swap or attractive static menu does not pass. The reviewed product must look
and behave like an RTS during an actual match.
