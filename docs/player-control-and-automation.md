# Player control and automation

The game should reduce repetitive execution while preserving the player's decisions. An accepted order gives a unit a task; it should keep doing that task until it completes, becomes impossible, or the player replaces it. Automatic behavior must not silently select a different strategy or consume an unchosen purchase.

This interpretation follows the authoritative [gameplay design](../outputs/frontline-command-game-design.md), especially gathering (§3.2), construction (§4.1), reliable orders (§5), aircraft service (§7), and repair (§16.1). It describes human-controlled forces. Bots make their own strategic decisions within the same legal costs, sight and command rules.

| Activity | Automatic execution | Player decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gathering | Choose available known fields and owned delivery centers, join loading queues, carry cargo home, repeat, and search known alternatives after depletion. | Pin a field or delivery center independently, release a pin, stop or redirect a worker. Retreat when attacked is an explicit preference, off by default. |
| Construction | Travel to an accepted foundation, build it, retain the assignment during short reroutes and report confirmed blockage. | Choose and pay for the building and footprint. Another rig resumes only when explicitly ordered; rigs do not choose new purchases or take over unrelated jobs. |
| Production and research | Start and finish the player's queued purchases when prerequisites, funds and capacity permit. | Choose units/upgrades, queue them and cancel them. No automatic replacement buying for human players. |
| Combat | Acquire legal visible targets within the chosen stance, fire with the role's normal movement/setup rules, and resume accepted later orders. | Choose Move, Attack, Attack-move, Guard, Hold or Aggressive; choose a named target or protected ally. Move does not become a chase. Hold does not move automatically. |
| Support | Apply canonical nearby healing/repair while eligible. Free medic healing stays free; paid mechanical repair obeys the player's credit reserve. Complete or invalid named repair releases only that front task. | Choose a named repair recipient, set the reserve and explicitly deploy or move a support vehicle. No distant automatic patient chase. |
| Aircraft | Return on empty ammunition or the endurance threshold, service, and resume authorized continuing duties or queued orders. | Choose targets, Patrol/Escort, Rebase and Repeat sortie. A one-off ground attack does not repeat unless explicitly enabled. |
| Paths and queues | Reroute briefly, retain task reservations until confirmed failure, visibly report blocked intent, and activate the next accepted task after completion or cancellation. | Choose destinations and queue with Shift/remapped controls. A new immediate order overrides automatic behavior. Invalid front work must not erase unrelated later orders. |
| Transport and capture | Approach and resolve an accepted boarding, unloading, capture, salvage or safehouse transfer task using current legality checks. | Choose passengers, carrier, destination and capture target. Successful boarding follows containment rules; a passenger's old queue is not an implicit disembark itinerary. |
| Powers and strategic operations | Regenerate charges/Energy and resolve a cast already accepted by the game. | Choose the ability, source, target and expenditure. A ready ability does not launch itself for a human player. |

Controls should show actual state and intent: cargo is separate from spendable credits; recent income uses simulation time; congestion counts need an honest observation scope; blocked destinations stay visible. Repeat sortie and retreat preferences need visible on/off states. Temporary sabotage makes power inactive even if the player requested Enable, so power status must distinguish effective operation from that request.

Enabled worker retreat applies while the current task is Gather. Immediate Hold, Stop, Move, boarding or another explicit task ends the active safety return and prevents new automatic retreats during that task. The preference stays visible and enabled, so a later Gather resumes the chosen automation. Turning the preference Off immediately ends an active safety return and resumes the retained gathering task without discarding cargo or later accepted orders. A delivery-center pin survives temporary inactivity; its actual delivery route may use another eligible center until the pinned center operates again.

Known fields show the player's last observation rather than current stock behind fog. Recent earned income includes deliveries, station payouts, salvage and scripted awards; label it as observed income over the shown simulation-time window, including partial windows. It is not a prediction or a worker-only income rate.

For a new player, keep the required decisions visible: what to build, what to produce, where to go and whom to fight. Explain optional repeat/retreat preferences beside their controls and show the currently accepted task. Automatic execution should have a visible reason and a reliable override. Difficulty changes bot decisions within legal rules; it should not secretly change the human player's controls or buy things for them.
